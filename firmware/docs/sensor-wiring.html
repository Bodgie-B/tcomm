Wiring the temperature sensor

Four wires: from the I2C group on the panel's green terminal to the four pins on your SHT40 board. Do it with the panel unplugged. Once you plug the panel back in, I'll flash the firmware that reads the sensor (after you confirm the port) and check the readings over serial.

The four connections

Panel terminalSensor pinSuggested wire
VOUT (3.3 V)VIN, VCC, V+ or 3V3Red
GND (the one next to VOUT)GNDBlack
SDASDABlue
SCLSCLYellow

Any colors work. These are the ones the common Qwiic and STEMMA QT sensor cables use, so if your sensor came with one of those cables, the colors already match. If yours differ, write down which color went where.

What you need

  • The SHT40 sensor board.
  • Four wires, 22 to 26 AWG, ideally no longer than about 50 cm (20 in). Shorter is better.
  • A wire stripper and a small flat screwdriver (2 to 2.5 mm blade) for the green terminal.
  • For the sensor end: whatever its pins take (female jumper wires, a soldering iron, or the sensor's cable).
  • Optional: a multimeter, for the 3.3 V check in step 6.
Only VOUT for power

The SHT40 runs on 3.3 V and is rated for 3.6 V at most. VOUT is the panel's 3.3 V supply. Never use the 5V or VIN positions for the sensor; 5 V or the 6–36 V input would destroy it.

Wire by wire

Step by step

  1. Keep the panel unplugged

    Wire with the USB-C cable out. Reconnect only after step 5.

  2. Read the sensor's labels

    Find the four names printed beside its pins: power (VCC, VIN, V+ or 3V3), GND, SDA and SCL. If the board has anything else (such as 3Vo or ADR), leave it unconnected.

  3. Strip the panel ends

    Strip about 6 mm (¼ in) of insulation from one end of each wire. If the wire is stranded, twist the strands tight so none stray, or crimp a small ferrule on.

  4. Fasten them in the green terminal

    Loosen the screw for each position, push the bare end fully into its opening, and tighten it. Then tug each wire gently: it should not move. Use positions 3 to 6 counted from the VIN end, which the case label marks as the I2C group: red into VOUT, black into the GND just above it, blue into SDA, yellow into SCL.

    The green block may pull straight off its pins, which makes wiring at the bench easier. If it doesn't come away with a firm, even pull, wire it in place.

  5. Connect the sensor end

    Match by name: red to the power pin, black to GND, blue to SDA, yellow to SCL. Check that no stray strand touches a neighbouring pin or screw at either end.

  6. Optional: check for 3.3 V

    If you have a multimeter, plug the panel in and measure on the terminal, black probe on GND: VOUT and SDA should each read about 3.3 V.

    If SDA reads about 5 V

    Unplug and stop. A jumper inside the case (marked H8, "I2C 3V3 / 5V") sets that voltage. It's set to 3.3 V at the factory, so this is unlikely, but 5 V on SDA would damage the sensor. Tell me and we'll change it together.

  7. Plug in and tell me

    Plug the panel back in and tell me it's on COM5. I'll flash the firmware with the sensor code and read its report over serial. Within a second of starting, the INSIDE reading on your floor's tile shows the real temperature and humidity. (It's the tile for the floor set in Settings > This unit is, which still says DOWNSTAIRS until you change it.) Breathe on the sensor: the humidity should climb within about 20 seconds.

If the touch screen stops respondingThe sensor shares its two signal wires with the touch controller. A short from SDA or SCL to ground blocks both. Unplug, look for a stray strand, and try again.
