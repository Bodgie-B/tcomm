# Firmware architecture (phase 2)

The firmware is one Arduino sketch, `tcomm/`, built with the pinned toolchain in the README. The handoff asked for application logic in modules with no hardware calls, so most of the code also builds on the PC, where it's tested and used to render screenshots.

| Folder | What's in it | Runs on |
|---|---|---|
| `src/core/` | Status, timer and Undo; doorbell sender and receiver; shared dog value; radio message format, duplicate filter and retry queue; sunrise, sunset and sky phases; backlight policy; sound synthesis; calendar maths; the household calendar's event store, repeats, its merge rules and file format (`events.*`); US federal holidays (`holidays.*`). No Arduino, ESP-IDF or LVGL headers. | panel and PC |
| `src/app/` | `Controller` ties the core together and builds a plain `View` for the screen. `Platform` is the interface to everything hardware-shaped. `Demo` runs a second Controller as a simulated other panel plus a fake gateway, over a loopback radio. `PanelApp` turns touches into controller calls. | panel and PC |
| `src/gfx/` | A small anti-aliased rasterizer that draws flat shapes into LVGL-format image buffers. | panel and PC |
| `src/ui/` | The LVGL 8.4 interface: theme colors, artwork (sky, tiles, badges, icons), main screen, sheets, Settings, the calendar roll-down (`ui_calendar.cpp`). Draws a `View`; sends touches to `ui::Actions`. | panel and PC |
| `src/board/` | I2C helpers under one lock that return errors instead of rebooting, the expander (backlight, amp), the PCF85063A clock, NVS storage, the calendar's files on the SD card (the FAT partition without one), the audio task, ESP-NOW, and `DevicePlatform`. | panel |
| `src/ws/` | Waveshare's display, touch, LVGL-port and codec drivers, unchanged except the LVGL task stack (6 KB to 16 KB). | panel |
| `tests/` | Host tests for core and controller, including two controllers talking over a fake radio. | PC |
| `sim/` | The screenshot simulator: LVGL on the PC drawing into memory. | PC |

## Tasks on the panel

Waveshare's LVGL task (core 1) draws the screen, reads touch, and runs the 50 ms app tick. The tick drains received radio frames, ticks the Controller and the Demo, and renders the View. Because the application runs inside the LVGL task, it needs no locks of its own; serial commands take the LVGL lock before calling in.

The audio task (core 0) synthesizes the sounds once at start-up, then plays queued requests, switching the amp on only while one plays.

In phase 3 the ESP-NOW receive callback, which runs in ESP-IDF's Wi-Fi task, puts frames on a queue for the app tick.

## Radio

`core/protocol.*` is the wire format from the handoff: a 5-byte header (version, type, sender, sequence), then the payload, all under 250 bytes. The `RetryQueue` sends up to 5 tries at 0, 150, 400, 750 and 1150 ms and gives up at 1.5 s, which marks the other panel No signal. Messages where only the latest matters (STATE, DOG, READING, OUTSIDE, EVENT_SUM, MAIL, REQUESTS) replace an older pending copy. MAIL is the mail badge (Mike, 2026-10-26, `core/mail.h`): upstairs marks mail for downstairs or takes it back, downstairs acknowledges it, and like a dog it carries a change counter so the later change wins; the panels exchange it at boot and after an outage once it has ever been used. Both panels must run firmware that knows MAIL: one that does not cannot ACK it, which marks the other panel No signal. COMFORT (Mike, 2026-11-06, `core/comfort.h`) carries one comfort-survey answer from downstairs to upstairs, which appends the identical line to its own `comfort.csv`; it is not a latest-wins type, so the downstairs panel keeps up to 32 unACKed answers in RAM, sends at most 2 at a time, and sends the rest again when upstairs is next heard after No signal or a boot. COMFORT_DEL (Mike, 2026-11-06, 12 bytes: answer number, removal time, UTC offset) tells upstairs the tenant removed a mistaken answer from the comfort chart so it marks the same line removed; it is not latest-wins and shares the answers' outbox, and goes out only once everything before it is ACKed so it never overtakes its own answer. REQUESTS (Mike, 2026-11-06, `core/requests.h`) carries the downstairs tenant's heat and cooling arrows (up to 3, a 32-byte fixed message): it is latest-wins like MAIL with a change counter, exchanged at boot and after an outage once used; arrows age out of the view after 4 hours from their send times, so nothing is sent when one times out. UPDATE (Mike, 2026-11-07, one reserved byte) is the Both panels button of Settings > Update over Wi-Fi: it asks the other panel to start its Wi-Fi updater (about 700 ms after the ACK, so the ACK gets out first), and the sender starts its own once the ACK arrives or after 2 s without one; it is not latest-wins and is acted on from the other panel, and from the gateway on the upstairs panel only (Mike, 2026-11-07: the Pi sends the gateway the serial line `UPDATE`, the gateway sends this message to upstairs, which does what its own Both panels button does, so no touch is needed on either panel; downstairs ignores it from the gateway; see `../gateway/docs/gateway-c6.md` and `docs/wifi-updates.md`). NOTE (Mike, 2026-11-07, `core/note.h`, 5 + 2 + text + 16 bytes) is a floor's tile message, at most 64 bytes of text, shown in place of the status line on that floor's tile on both panels: latest-wins per floor with a change counter, only the floor that wrote it changes it, but both floors' messages are exchanged at boot and after an outage once used, and each panel keeps a copy of the other floor's: a NOTE about a panel's own floor with a higher counter (its saved state was lost) is adopted, and when ours is the newer one it is sent back, as with MAIL; a NOTE from the other panel never counts as a touch (it must not wake the screen), and it goes by its own end time so nothing is sent when it times out; upstairs clearing the arrows clears its message, and downstairs shows the acted text in that spot only while the upstairs message is not newer than the acted time (a message Mike writes after clearing the arrows shows). The downstairs comfort outbox is also saved to flash on every change and restored at start-up, so a restart loses no answer or removal. EVENT carries one calendar event; EVENT_SUM a summary of the whole calendar, sent when the panels find each other (see `docs/calendar.md`). The receiver ACKs everything, duplicates included, and drops repeats by (sender, sequence). Entries expire after 10 s and each boot starts at a random sequence number, so a rebooted sender isn't mistaken for a duplicate.

### The clock and the outside history (Mike, 2026-11-07)

The WeatherGate sends internet-verified time with every weather reading (the OUTSIDE message's time field), so the panels no longer join the home Wi-Fi to ask pool.ntp.org after a power cut. A panel takes that time as `kClockNetwork` whenever its clock is not set or is more than 2 s off in either direction, never a time below `kClockFloor` (2026-01-01: the Pi sends 0 until it has synced); network time wins over a time set by hand and over a copy from the other panel. Upstairs passes the reading on to downstairs with the time brought up to date (a reading passed on again minutes later carries the time of the pass, not of the first receipt). The Wi-Fi updater and setting the time by hand stay.

Every 15 minutes (local :00, :15, :30, :45, in the first minute of the quarter, once per quarter; the last quarter written is kept in RAM only) each panel with a valid clock and a fresh outside reading appends one line to that local day's file `oYYYYMMDD.csv` in `/tcomm` on the SD card (`core/outside_log.*`: `date,time,utc,temp_f,rh,forecast,flags`), forever. A quarter with no fresh reading is a gap. The chart that reads the files back (a long press on the Outside tile) is in `ui/ui_outside.cpp`.

## What the PC can and can't check

The PC runs the same core, controller, demo and interface code. It can't check display timing, touch, real ESP-NOW, the codec, the RTC, NVS, task stacks or memory pressure on the ESP32. Those wait for the board.
